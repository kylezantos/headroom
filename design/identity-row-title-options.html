The account title in a provider card

"Personal" and "Work" currently sit at 15pt semibold, primary ink, directly above meter labels at 13pt medium at 90% ink. Two points of size and one step of weight is a thin margin, so the name reads louder than a piece of card furniture should while still not being the loudest thing in the card. Ten ways below to make the row read as identity without asking for attention.

The options are composable. Approving A and I together is a real answer, so is approving E on its own. Nothing here is exclusive except where a note says two options touch the same number.

The tension

Three tiers, two of them adjacent

What is actually competing

A provider card holds three kinds of text, and only two of them are far enough apart to read as different jobs.

  • Account title15pt semibold, kerning −0.15, primary ink. ProviderCardIdentityRows.swift:80-87
  • Meter label13pt medium at 90% ink. UsageBarView.swift:22, :166-167
  • Numeral18pt semibold monospaced, primary ink. UsageBarView.swift:24, :204-207

The numeral is clearly the loudest thing, and it should be. The problem is the pair above it: the title and the label are one weight step apart at two points of size difference, both effectively at full or near-full ink, so the title has to shout a little to be a different tier at all. Every option below either quiets the title, quiets the labels, or separates them by something other than type.

What does not move. The plan badge stays 9.5pt medium tertiary in a stepBackground capsule except where an option says otherwise, the mark keeps its brand colour, and the "API spend" strip under every mock stays at its shipped 11pt semibold secondary so you have one fixed reference point to judge each title against.

Ten options · s-A to s-J

Distinguish the row without raising its voice

Every mock is one Claude card with two accounts, drawn at 1px = 1pt from the tokens: Personal (Max) with Weekly 94%, 5-hour 93% and Weekly (Fable) 95%, then Work (Team) with 98%, 100% and 97%. Each option is shown twice, Micro segmented on the left because that is your daily setup, Full chip glass on the right because that is where the type has the most room to be wrong. Use the Dark and Light toggle in the bar above.

Today Baseline · what ships on the branch Not an option. The measuring stick for everything below. title 15 semibold −0.15 primary · badge 9.5 · gap 7 · sections 22

Changes: nothing. This is d64a0f6d as drawn from the tokens.

Effect: the title reads as a heading over its rows, and at a glance the eye lands on the name before the numbers on the same line.

Cost: two accounts in one card means two headings competing with six meter labels in 250pt of height.

A Weight down Same size, one weight step lighter. title 15 medium −0.15 primary · everything else unchanged

Changes: the title drops from semibold to medium and nothing else moves.

Effect: the title and the meter labels now share a weight, so the two points of size difference carry the whole distinction on their own.

Cost: at 15pt medium over 13pt medium the name can read soft rather than quiet, especially in light mode where the card is nearly white.

B Size down One point smaller, and a little more air below. title 14 semibold −0.1 primary · gap 7 → 9

Changes: the title drops to 14pt with slightly looser tracking, and the identity row sits 9pt above the meters instead of 7pt.

Effect: the size gap to the labels narrows to one point, so the extra 2pt of air does the separating that size used to do.

Cost: a two-account card grows 4pt taller, and 14 semibold over 13 medium is a very fine distinction to hold at a glance.

C Ink down Full size and weight, less than full ink. title 15 semibold at 80% dark / 82% light

Changes: the title keeps its size and weight but steps off primary ink to white at 80% in dark and #1D1F24 at 82% in light.

Effect: the 18pt numerals become the only full-primary text in the card, so the numbers own the top of the hierarchy without anything else shrinking.

Cost: 80% title against 90% label ink is a 10 point difference, which can collapse into one tier on a bright display or under increased contrast.

D Section label Stop treating the name as a heading. title 13 semibold secondary · badge unchanged · gap 6

Changes: the title drops to 13pt at secondary ink, #EBEBF5 at 66% dark and #3D3D42 at 84% light, and the row tightens to 6pt above the meters.

Effect: the name reads as a group label sitting on top of its rows rather than a heading announcing them, and the plan badge stays the only chrome in the row.

Cost: the title is now exactly the size of the meter labels, so only weight and ink separate them, and the account name loses its scan value in a five-account popover.

E Brand tint Separate by hue instead of by weight. title 14 semibold in provider text accent · mark unchanged

Changes: the title takes the provider text accent, Claude #E88F69 dark and #B3541C light, Codex #96A1FF and #3340D6, while the mark stays exactly as it is.

Effect: mark and name read as one branded unit and the neutral meter labels sit visibly apart from it, so the tiers separate by colour rather than by size.

Cost: two accounts of one provider repeat the same coloured name twice in one card, and this breaks the KTD9 rule that the mark owns the only brand colour in the row.

F Tracked small label Small and slightly tracked, never caps. title 12.5 semibold +0.2 secondary · badge 9 · gap 6

Changes: the title shrinks to 12.5pt with 0.2 of tracking at secondary ink, the plan badge follows down to 9pt, and the row tightens to 6pt above the meters.

Effect: the row reads as a quiet label strip, clearly below the meter labels in size, with the tracking keeping short names from looking cramped.

Cost: it converges with the 11pt "API spend" header, so two different kinds of section title start to look like the same thing in one scroll.

G Mark-led Let the icon carry identity and the text recede. mark 18 in an 18 slot · column 13 + 18 + 6 = 37 · title 14 medium

Changes: the provider mark grows from 15 to 18pt, the content column moves from 34 to 37pt on both edges to match, and the title drops to 14pt medium at primary ink.

Effect: the icon becomes the thing you scan for, and the account name reads as the caption beside it rather than the label above the rows.

Cost: every meter row indents 3pt further and the Full gauge loses 6pt of length, and this is the only option that changes L4B geometry.

H Labels lighter instead Change the rows below, not the title. title unchanged · labels 13 regular at 78% · countdown at 50%

Changes: the title stays exactly as it ships, and the meter labels drop from medium to regular at 78% ink with the countdown falling from 53% to 50% in dark and 72% to 50% in light.

Effect: the distinction arrives from below, so the title stops competing because the rows got quieter rather than because it got smaller.

Cost: six quieter labels per card is a bigger legibility change than one title, and a 50% countdown in light mode is close to the contrast floor.

I Airier header Distinction by space, not by type. type unchanged · gap 7 → 12 · sections 22 → 26

Changes: no type changes at all; the identity row gains 5pt of air below it and the two account sections move 4pt further apart.

Effect: the title separates by position instead of by contrast, so it stays a proper heading but stops crowding the rows it heads.

Cost: a two-account card grows 14pt taller, which pushes a four-provider popover into the 640pt scroll cap sooner.

J Metadata line Read the row as data, not as a title. title 14 semibold primary · email 10.5 tertiary inline · badge trailing

Changes: the title drops to 14pt, the account email sits inline right after it at 10.5pt tertiary and middle-truncates when it will not fit, and the plan badge moves to the row's trailing edge.

Effect: the row reads as a line of metadata rather than a heading, and the trailing badge anchors the same right edge the numerals already align to.

Cost: the email is optional per account, so a card mixing one row with an address and one without will look unbalanced.
