iOS companion

Tap the widget, land somewhere that was clearly built on purpose.

Headroom on the Mac already answers one question well: how much room is left before I hit a wall. The phone shouldn't try to be a second control panel — it should be a calm mirror of that same answer, sized for a glance and a thumb.

We've picked Mirror — the faithful full-picture port — and refined how it sits on the surface. Below: the interface in both themes, the widget hand-off, a light-touch settings take, and the states it has to survive.

02 · The interface

Mirror — the whole picture, both themes

We're taking Mirror forward: two provider tiles, both windows each, everything at once. Elevation is now carried by a layered ring-shadow in light and a restrained luminous edge in dark — the shadow does the border's job, so there are no hard outlines and no heavy drop shadows.

Dark
A faint white ring plus a top rim define each tile — a quiet glow, not an outline.
Light
A 4-layer ring-shadow (5%-black hairline + soft ambient) stands in for both the border and the drop shadow.
03 · Settings, on purpose small

A mirror, not a second cockpit

The Mac owns the real config — refresh cadence, API keys, renewal dates. The phone keeps only what's local to this device: appearance, which providers to show, the widget metric. Everything else is a pointer back to the Mac.

Light-touch settings
Sync status up top, three device-local toggles, and an honest “managed on your Mac” line.
…or no settings at all
The fork worth naming: since the phone only mirrors, the entire screen above can collapse into a small About sheet — sync status, version, “manage in Headroom on your Mac,” done. Appearance follows the system; providers follow whatever the Mac syncs.

Recommendation: ship the About sheet first. Add the three toggles only if people actually want a phone that shows less than their Mac.

Design notes

  • One system, ported not reinvented. Glass tiles (R17), the health ramp, provider accents, plan badges, tabular numerals and reset labels are lifted straight from the redesigned popover.
  • Health colors the bars; brand colors the identity. Claude clay and Codex blue only ever touch the logo and plan pill — the meter is always green/amber/red, so severity reads at a glance.
  • Motion conveys state, never decoration. Bars and vessels animate their fill on open and on refresh; no idle pulses, no looping glows — matching the app's own no-pulse rule.
  • Adapted, not copy-pasted. The iPhone has room the menu bar doesn't: larger type, roomier meters, pull-to-refresh, safe areas. Error copy is re-pointed from CLI commands to “on your Mac.”
  • Elevation without borders. Each tile is defined by a layered ring-shadow in light (a 5%-black hairline ring + three soft ambient layers) and a restrained light glow in dark (a faint white ring + top rim) — no hard borders, no heavy drop shadows. Body text clears 4.5:1 in both.
  • Scope: Mirror is the interface we're building now; Vessels · Rings · Grid · Board are parked (code retained) — Vessels likely comes next.
