How Headroom should move

Fourteen decisions. Three of them set the system — the curve family, what Reduce Motion means, and how hover and press are timed. Eleven are moments: the specific instants where something in the app changes and we have to decide whether it moves, and if so, how.

The weighting. Emil first: restraint, and "should this animate at all?" as the default question. Jakub second: production polish, exits subtler than enters, motion you feel rather than watch. Apple's fluid-interface rules govern springs (critically damped, interruptible, animate from the presentation value) and Reduce Motion (a gentler equivalent, not nothing). SwiftUI constrains what is even reachable — always .animation(_, value:), chain with withAnimation {} completion: {} rather than delays, and the menu-bar label is a rasterized NSImage that contentTransition cannot touch.

The standing rules. No pulsing, breathing or looping motion, ever. This is a glance app — you open it for a second and close it. Nothing exceeds 300ms. One cause produces one motion, not three that happen to start together.

Not up for debate

What already exists and is right

These five tokens and the decisions around them are the baseline. Every option below either uses them, extends them with a named role, or is explicit about proposing a sixth value. The inventory counts 48 animation call sites across the app target, 6 of which are not Reduce Motion gated — all six in the two setup-card button styles.

Plus one custom transition, settingsReveal (SettingsRows.swift:156-179): enter opacity 0→1 + 4pt rise + 1.5 blur, exit opacity only. And one session gate: the popover content fades once per app launch and is instant thereafter (PopoverView.swift:160-168 — "replaying motion there reads as latency").

The four gaps, ranked by how often you would see them

  1. The minute tick. The countdown on every meter row and "Updated N min ago" in the header both hard-cut once a minute while the popover is open. Inventory §4 #12 and #13.
  2. The menu-bar number. Changes on every poll, hard cut, because the label is a rasterized bitmap swapped whole (MenuBarLabel.swift:32-38). Inventory §4 #24.
  3. Meter style change. Bursty but high-attention: you audition five to ten combinations once. Nothing is keyed on meterStyle anywhere — the popover and the specimen both hard-cut, and the swatch ring has no animation at all. Inventory §4 #8.
  4. Settings pane switch. Hard cut, several times per visit (SettingsView.swift:188-259). Inventory §4 #17.

Trailing the four: the popover's own card list has no reorder or insert animation, while Settings' account list does (AccountsSettingsTile.swift:280). That is M7.

One fidelity note up front. Every mock is drawn at 1px = 1pt from the tokens, and the default meter is micro.chip.event. Under colorAsEvent the fill recipe resolves to .neutral when healthy and .solid otherwise, and the standalone cap marker is drawn only for the glass and ghost fills (MeterGauge.swift:101). So the default micro chip you see below correctly has no cap; the 2 × 7 r0 micro cap (L7B) appears in the M5 glass swatch where the recipe actually calls for it.
