See more accounts without scrolling

A user with seven Claude accounts (named Account 1 to Account 7) wrote in. Today's popover:

“fits like 5 accounts well but would be nice to see more without scrolling”

The popover is one 384pt column of provider cards. Past 640pt the cards scroll (PopoverView.swift:214). This doc lays out ways to use more width instead, each drawn to scale (1px = 1pt) from tokens resolved out of the code. A separate setting, Popover height: Compact / Fit all accounts, is being planned on its own. This doc only notes how it interacts with columns (HD-4).

The key insight. The person asking has many accounts inside one provider. Putting provider cards side by side (option A) does nothing for them: the Claude card is still one tall column with Codex sitting next to it, mostly empty. For this user, a single provider's accounts have to be able to flow into columns. Options B, C and E do that. D gets a similar gain by packing more rows into the same width.
01 · Today

What ships now

These are offscreen renders of the real PopoverView from swift run Headroom --render-popover on origin/main e04f6c3a. The harness renders every --demo scenario. It has no flag to pick one, so these were taken from the full run.

The numbers

Shell 384 wide, 19 margin, cards 346, account content 320 (PopoverCardMetrics, PopoverView.swift:756-758).

A two-row Micro account takes about 101pt including the 26pt account gap (measured from the multi-5 render). A three-row Claude account (with a Fable weekly row) takes about 133pt.

The 640pt cap holds about 6 two-row accounts, or about 5 once one has three rows. That's the user's “like 5”. The popover maxes out at about 764pt tall (measured).

13″ MacBook Air screens are 1470×956pt by default (M2 and later; M1 is 1440×900). That figure comes from Apple's published defaults and wasn't checked in this repo.

Harness limits. (1) It snapshots about 1.35s after refresh, while multi-20's demo Claude probes take 4s each, so the multi-20 capture is all placeholders. (2) Its isolated preferences leave Grok and Cursor switched off. That makes multi-4 render as Claude + Codex only, and multi-20 render as 20 Claude + 20 Codex instead of the 61-account envelope (20 Claude, 20 Codex, 20 Grok, 1 Cursor). Neither was changed for this doc.
02 · How to read the mocks

Fixtures, controls, honesty

The top bar switches every mock between three fixtures. 7 Claude + Codex is the person who wrote in. 4 providers, mixed has 3 Claude accounts (one waiting on sign-in, one with a Fable weekly row), 2 Codex (one stale), and 1 each of Grok and Cursor; this is a new fixture, since multi-mixed is Claude + Codex only. 20 accounts is 20 Claude + 1 Codex, the tested per-provider envelope landing in a single provider.

Titles → Emails swaps every account name for a long email address, to stress-test the planned “use account email as title” setting. Meter redraws every mock in another format × shape. Gauges are hand-drawn approximations of MeterGauge using the MeterGeometry sizes. Heights come from the browser laying out the token sizes, so expect about ±2pt against SwiftUI. Each caption gives the width and says whether the content fits under the 640pt cap or how much scrolls.

Options

A Provider columns Each provider card becomes a column. The popover widens. 750 · 1116 · (1482)

This is FlowingColumns as the iPad already has it, run at popover scale with whole provider cards as the units. Each card is 346 wide, so its contents don't change. With 4 providers, 4 columns would be 1482pt, which is wider than a 13″ Air, so the mock stops at 3 and the 4th card drops under the shortest column, the way FlowingColumnsPlanner.assign does.

Best for

People with 2–4 providers and a few accounts each. Doesn't help the person who asked: the Claude column still scrolls.

Width

38 + 346·k + 20·(k−1): 750 for 2, 1116 for 3, 1482 for 4, which doesn't fit a 1470pt screen.

Meter styles

Unchanged. Cards keep 346, so Full's 278pt run, segmented cells and Micro rows draw exactly as today.

Make Main / reorder

Make Main only reorders accounts within a card, so it stays a vertical move inside one column. The popover has no drag today; the pill-shelf drag (PillShelfLayout) is in Settings → Menu Bar.

Effort · risk

Low–medium. The planner is reused as-is: margin 0 (the shell already pads), gap 20, min/max 346. The layout reports “one card + margins” as its ideal width, and MenuBarExtra sizes its window to the ideal size, so the width has to be pinned. Popover anchoring when wide is unverified (HD-8).

Parity

Provider-agnostic. Leaves a gap on iPad too: iPad columns hold whole provider cards, so 7 Claude accounts there is also one tall card.
B Account grid inside the card Each provider card spans the popover. Its accounts sit in a 2 or 3 column grid. 724 · 1064

Today's account section is exactly 320pt wide (mark gutter, title, meters, 21pt trailing inset), so the grid reuses AccountSectionView unchanged. Accounts fill across, then down (Account 1, Account 2 on the first row). Gaps: 20 between columns, 26 between rows (today's account gap).

Best for

Many accounts in one or two providers, where every account looks alike (same rows, all live).

Width

Card 26 + 320·k + 20·(k−1), popover +38: 724 for 2 columns, 1064 for 3. About 26pt narrower than C, because all columns share one card's padding.

Meter styles

Unchanged at 320 per account. Weak spot: a grid row is as tall as its tallest account, so a 3-row or sign-in account leaves a gap beside it.

Make Main / reorder

The new main moves to the top-left. Accounts that shift move across columns and down rows, which needs layout-owned positions (the PillShelfLayout lesson) so it reads as a move and not a fade.

Effort · risk

Medium. Only multiAccountBody becomes a grid; the card chrome and the account section stay. A provider with one account (Grok, Cursor, most Codex users) still gets a full-width card with one tile in it, which wastes space (see the 4-provider fixture).

Parity

Works for all four. Cursor is always one tile. Grok and Codex extras tile like Claude.
C Flowing account columns Accounts run down a column, then into the next. A long provider splits across columns. 750 · 1116

Accounts are laid out like newspaper columns: Claude's accounts run down column 1 and carry on at the top of column 2, then Codex follows. The card edge is the break between providers. Every account row already carries its provider mark in multi-account mode, so a continued card needs no “Claude (cont.)” label. The split points are picked so the columns come out as even as possible, with a small penalty for splitting a provider.

Best for

Everyone. It handles one provider with many accounts and many providers with one account each, and uneven account heights don't leave gaps.

Width

38 + 346·k + 20·(k−1): 750 for 2 columns, 1116 for 3. Each column is today's card width, so nothing inside a card changes.

Meter styles

Unchanged. Full rows make accounts taller, and the balancing simply uses the real heights.

Make Main / reorder

The main stays at the top-left. A promotion pushes one account from the bottom of column 1 to the top of column 2, so positions must be layout-owned, as in FlowingColumnsLayout and PillShelfLayout. Keep the planner's rule of re-planning only when the order, width or style changes. A height change (a sign-in row appearing) should restack in place and never shuffle accounts between columns while you're reading.

Effort · risk

Medium–high. Reusable: FlowingColumnsGeometry (count and width), the plan-keeping rule, the layout-owned placement pattern. New: a pure column-major partition in Core (testable like FlowingColumnsPlanner), and splitting a provider into card fragments. Card-level state then belongs to the first fragment only (capability notice, Stop Waiting). Run --bench-ui before and after.

Parity

Treats all four providers alike. The same planner could later replace iPad's whole-card flow, which has the same one-tall-Claude-card problem.
D Dense rows One line per account at today's width. About 3× the density, no width change. 384 · 750 for 20

Not a columns layout, but the strongest alternative to one. Each account is one 26pt line: mark, title (shortened in the middle), then a small cell per window with gauge and number. Window names sit once in a header row above each card. Reset countdowns move to the tooltip. Extra windows (Fable, Spark) show as a “+1” label. All sizes here are proposals: title 13 semibold, numeral 15, cell 80 with 12 between cells, row 26, gap 4. Gauges keep MeterGeometry's Micro sizes.

Best for

Power users with 10–20 accounts who scan for “which one has room”, not reset times. Keeps the narrow popover close to its icon.

Width

384 (unchanged) up to about 18 rows. 20+ accounts needs a second column (750), shown on the 20-account fixture.

Meter styles

Can't fully honour the user's meter style. Full has no room on a line, so it draws as its Micro twin. Tube fits. The percentage word (“left”) moves into the header. That's a real cost to a global setting.

Make Main / reorder

Same as today: a vertical move within a card.

Effort · risk

Medium. A new shared row component (in SharedUI, so the phone could use it). Loses reset times, plan labels and stale reasons from view. Titles get about 95pt, so most emails are cut in the middle.

Parity

Claude, Codex and Grok fit two cells. Cursor's “Cursor Models / Other Models” fit in 80pt. Claude's Fable and Codex Spark rows become “+1”, so some numbers are less visible on those two providers.
E Automatic: columns only when it would scroll A rule, not a setting. Uses C's flow. 384 → 750 → 1116

Use the fewest columns (1, 2, then 3) that keep the tallest column under 640pt. Past 3 columns, the popover scrolls as it does today. Five accounts look exactly like today, and the popover only widens for people who would otherwise scroll. The column count is decided when the popover opens and when accounts are added, removed or reordered, never while it's open, so a row changing height can't make the width jump.

Best for

A sensible default for everyone. People with few accounts never see a change.

Width

Follows C: 384, 750 or 1116. Could be capped at 2 columns (HD-3, HD-8).

Meter styles

As C. A Full style makes accounts taller, so it reaches 2 columns sooner. That's expected.

Make Main / reorder

As C. A reorder doesn't change the total height, so it never changes the column count by itself.

Effort · risk

C, plus a small pure rule in Core. Risk: adding a sixth account doubles the popover's width. That's intended, but it's a jump the user didn't ask for. A setting to force one column (HD-1) covers people who dislike it.

Parity

Counts every provider's accounts alike.
04 · Where a wide popover lands

Footprint on a 13″ MacBook Air

Drawn at 0.3× on a 1470pt-wide screen. The menu bar icon is assumed to be at x≈1180, a typical spot for third-party status items. I assumed the popover centres under the icon and gets pushed back on-screen, 8pt from the edge. That's unverified: how MenuBarExtra(.window) positions a 750 or 1116pt window needs one check in the real menu bar (see HD-8).

Two columns still read as “a menu under my icon”. Three columns covers most of the screen and feels like a window. That's the case for capping at 2 (HD-3) or allowing 3 only when the screen is wide enough.

05 · Considered and set aside

Rejected candidates

One line each. Approve one if you'd like it reconsidered.

HR-1Horizontal scrolling strip

Mouse wheels scroll vertically, and nothing in a menu bar popover suggests sideways scrolling. Off-screen accounts are just as hidden as they are today.

HR-2Tabs or a provider picker

Hides the comparison across everything at a glance, which is the popover's job, and adds a click to every look.

HR-3Collapse extra accounts behind a disclosure

Swaps scrolling for clicking. The hidden numbers are the ones the user asked to see.

HR-4Shrink the type to fit

Breaks the tuned type scale and the no-minimumScaleFactor rule, and gets less readable exactly when there's more to read.

HR-5Resizable or detachable window

MenuBarExtra windows aren't user-resizable. A pinned dashboard window is a separate feature with its own lifecycle, out of scope here.

06 · Open decisions

What needs a call

My recommendation is in green. Each can be approved, denied or discussed independently.

HD-1Setting or automatic?
  • Automatic only (E).
  • A setting only: Single column / Columns.
  • Automatic by default, plus a setting: Layout: Automatic / Single column.

Recommend the third. Automatic changes nothing for most people, and the setting is an escape hatch for anyone who dislikes the wider popover.

HD-2Where it lives in Settings

Appearance has the specimen, Theme, Meter style, and the In-app display tile (percentages, account title ink, Spark counters) (SettingsPanes.swift:203-239). I'd add a Popover layout row to In-app display as thumbnail swatches, like Theme. The planned height setting could sit beside it. The specimen stays one card at 346pt: it previews a card's contents, not the layout.

Recommend Appearance → In-app display, swatch row, next to the height setting.

HD-3Column count rule
  • Column width is always 346 (today's card), so nothing inside a card ever reflows.
  • Use the fewest columns that remove scrolling. Maximum: 2 (750pt) or 3 (1116pt).
  • Decide when the popover opens and when accounts are added, removed or reordered. Never decide while it's open.

Recommend a maximum of 2. Three only if the live anchoring check (HD-8) shows a 1116pt popover still reads as belonging to its icon.

HD-4How columns and “Fit all accounts” height combine

Both settings fight scrolling. Proposed order: columns first, then height. Compact keeps the 640pt cap and scrolls once columns run out. Fit all grows the height up to the screen after the maximum columns are used. With Layout set to Single column, Fit all just grows the one column. The other order (grow height first, then add columns) keeps the popover narrow but makes it taller than the 764pt that was tuned, and tall is slower to scan than wide.

Recommend columns first, then height.

HD-5Long email titles

The planned “use account email as title” setting makes titles 25–45 characters long. Today the title is shortened at the end (IdentityRow.swift:219-220), so “studio-billing@northwind-res…” loses the domain. Proposal: when the title is an email, shorten it in the middle (“studio-bil…example.com”), as the persistent email line already does (IdentityRow.swift:143-144). The tooltip keeps the full address. Turn on Titles → Emails in the top bar to see it in every mock. Today's baseline keeps end-truncation for comparison.

Recommend middle truncation for email titles, on every surface that shows a title.

HD-6Reading order in columns
  • Down, then across (newspaper, C): the main account stays top-left, a provider's accounts stay together, and uneven heights pack tightly.
  • Across, then down (grid rows, B): rows line up, but each row is as tall as its tallest account.

Recommend down, then across.

HD-7API Spend card in columns

The spend card follows the provider cards today (PopoverView.swift:156). In columns it can be the last item in the flow (it lands at the bottom of the last column), or a full-width strip under all columns. None of the fixtures includes it.

Recommend the last item in the flow, so it's balanced like everything else.

HD-8Verify anchoring before committing to width

How macOS places a MenuBarExtra(.window) that's wider than the space to the right of its icon is assumed, not measured. It needs one short live check with a stub popover at 750 and 1116, in both light and dark. It needs your go-ahead, because it drives the real menu bar.

Recommend running this check before a plan locks the maximum column count.
