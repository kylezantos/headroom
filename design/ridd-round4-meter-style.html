Headroom · design feedback · round 4 · rev 5 · Aug 2026

Meter style, opened up

Round 3 swept the micro-gauge’s variables and the reaction wasn’t "pick one" — it was "I sort of like all of these." So this round reframes shape and fill as a user preference and tests whether the combinations hold together. Rev 3 settled the visual frame; rev 4 turned it into a decision record and drew a boundary around the original full-width gauges. Rev 5 renders that boundary: one expanded-layout candidate, tested against the micro default instead of reopening the entire matrix.

The turn · Kyle, Aug 28

"What I’m starting to think is that I sort of like all these options — I want to give users the ability to choose any one of these for the display of their meter. You pick your shape of the meter, and then your color preference. That might be cool."

The rev-3 review · Kyle, Aug 28

"I can’t really tell if I like the proud fill or not — I’m leaning against it. Labels at 90%. Countdown alone, D2 brightness. The weekly label and the 3d 23h next to it don’t look center-aligned. And light mode just falls a little flat — we need another audit of those colors."

Both paraphrased from Kyle’s voice notes; the Ridd tactility quote behind the proud-fill thread is verbatim in round 1 ("the line had a shadow to it and was slightly bigger than the actual bar container", 01:13–01:24).

The cost of choice
What customization commits us to

Every shipped combo has to hold up in both schemes, in the widgets, at three severities, and through the freshness states (stale, unavailable) — the matrix above appears twice in this document for exactly that reason. The rev-2 launch set is four shapes (chip, machined, ticks, tube) × four colors (solid, glass + cap, color as event, hue ramp) — sixteen combos, fifteen clean and one flagged (ticks × ramp) that ships only if the stepped version proves out. Arc and ring stay parked, not deleted. A default still has to be chosen for first-run: chip + color-as-event, per the round-3 verdict.

Tactility, the verdict

The proud fill: recommend cutting it

Kyle leans against; this doc agrees, for a structural reason rather than a taste one. Ridd’s ask was born in round 1 on the full-width bar, where a proud fill has room to read as an object. Shrunk to a 32pt chip there is no good dose: at 1.5pt of overhang the shadows accumulate into texture, at 1pt the effect is nearly subliminal — machinery without a payoff. It also taxes every other axis: each of the four shapes needs its own proud answer, light mode amplifies the cast shadows, and the style matrix doubles its render states. The tactility Ridd wanted survives where it’s structural, not decorative: the machined well already delivers "you could touch it" through its recessed channel, with zero shadows on the fill. Flat fills everywhere else. The three variants stay below as the record — and the way back in, if the flat build feels sterile on the live surface.

The other scheme

Light mode, audited

Rev 2’s light translation used the app’s existing light tokens verbatim and fell flat — everything read gray, brown, and vaguely disabled. An OKLCH audit found three causes, none of them "the colors are too dark": the fills kept their dark-mode 85% alpha, so they composited toward the gray track and drained the chroma (the amber was landing at a muddy #c17c3a); "glass" was plain black-alpha — a gray fill on a gray track at the same hue is the exact recipe of a disabled slider; and amber can’t stay amber that dark — at the lightness the 3:1 track contrast demands, hue 67° has lost a third of its chroma, so it drifts 8.7° toward orange (inside the 10° drift threshold) where the gamut is wider. The rebuilt fills hold every hue from the dark tokens, sit at their maximum lightness that still clears 3:1 vs the track, and render opaque; glass becomes a cool steel gradient. These are a proposal for a light fill token set in BrandColorValues.swift — the 4.5:1 text tokens are untouched.

Decision record

Landed choices

This is the build-facing summary. Earlier sections keep the rejected variants and reasoning; this section is the one place to read what actually survived.

Frame and hierarchy

  • Gauge before the numeral in the right-edge reading cluster.
  • Window labels at 90%; the numeral remains primary through size and weight.
  • Bare countdown at D2: 53% dark / 72% light, measuring 4.63:1 / 4.62:1 worst-case at 11px.
  • Label + countdown and numeral + unit are baseline-aligned clusters.
  • The tooltip retains the full “Resets in …” sentence for cold-read ambiguity.

Meter system

  • Default: chip + color-as-event.
  • Shape choices: chip, machined, ticks, tube.
  • Color choices: solid, glass + cap, color-as-event, hue ramp.
  • Shape and color remain independent preferences; “left” vs “used” remains separate.
  • Widgets follow the selected meter language only after each combination proves out there.

Material and color

  • Flat fills ship. Proud/overfilled treatments stay in the record, not the recommendation.
  • The machined shape carries tactility structurally through its recessed well.
  • Light fills are scheme-specific and opaque; “glass” becomes cool steel rather than black-alpha gray.
  • Every recipe preserves the green → amber → red severity ramp in both schemes.

2026-09-03 — severity fills split from severity text. The rev-3 proposal above (a light severityFill set audited to 3:1 on the track) shipped as the darkened light tokens, and in the app those bars read muddy. Product decision: in the default light appearance the bar is a redundant visualization — the percent numeral beside it already states the value at full text contrast — so the fills go vivid and sit at 2.06–3.22:1 on the track instead of 3:1. Accessibility is held by two rules: all text, icons, and the “Low” pill stay on the (unchanged) text tokens, and Increase Contrast swaps the light fills back to the 3:1-passing values. Ratios and the reasoning live in HeadroomSeverityColorValues + BrandColorValuesTests. Note for future rounds: the light track stays at rgba(0,0,0,0.12) — darkening it reduces fill separation, because these mid-luminance fills are darker than the track and converge on it.

Parked and gated

  • Ticks × ramp remains an experiment; gate it on the low-percentage collapse.
  • Arc and ring stay parked until cap and path-gradient behavior have clean answers.
  • Multi-account density remains the next proof: same-provider stacks and the 640pt cap.
  • Build the frame first, then the flat default, then expose the picker once at least two styles are real.
Recommendation · still open

Don’t bring back a full-width copy of every micro gauge

The old full gauges are the ancestors of this matrix, but restoring all of them would duplicate the system on the wrong axis. Full width changes row height, scanning behavior, and multi-account density; that makes it an expanded layout, not a fifth shape. The one candidate worth a fair return is the full-width recessed instrument + color-as-event: K’s continuous length read with T’s quiet healthy state.

Micro familyFull-width ancestorRecommendation
SolidToday / V · lit tube, full colorDo not restore; the color mass competes with the numeral again.
Glass + capI / S · ghost or grey-glass fill, bright capKeep as a fill recipe; full width adds footprint without a new reading advantage.
Color as eventB / T · healthy neutral, amber/red interruptUse inside the single expanded candidate; it is the calmest full-width behavior.
Hue rampH / W · hue travelKeep micro for now; full width restores too much ambient color.
TicksJ · segmented meterKeep micro; at full width it reads like dashboard telemetry.
MachinedK · recessed instrumentTest this one. It uniquely regains continuous length-at-a-glance and makes depth earn its space.

Fair test: compare the current micro default against K × T at one, two, and five accounts in both schemes. That comparison is rendered immediately below. If the full-width version cannot justify its height at five accounts, keep it out of the preference model rather than adding a density toggle nobody asked for.

This is not a fifth meter shape. It is a denser-versus-more-spatial layout choice: the current micro chip + color-as-event against an 8px recessed instrument + color-as-event.
