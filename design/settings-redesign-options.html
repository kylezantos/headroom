Settings redesign options

The meter style system landed a real set of visual choices (format, shape, fill), and the Settings window has not caught up. It is eight peer tiles in one 700pt scroll, with the visual decisions scattered across three of them and nothing on screen that shows what a combination actually looks like. Eight layout options below, plus a live component gallery of the picker parts, all drawn on the shipped tokens at 1px = 1pt.

FIXED

Tile materialRaised tile, 13pt pad, 10pt radius, top-lit rim, real shadow.

FIXED

Row grammarLabel leading, control trailing. Switches for masters, checkboxes for dependents.

FIXED

Native controlsSystem segmented pickers, small switches, standard push buttons. No custom widgets.

FIXED

No pulsingStatus is drawn, never animated. Motion is hover and press only.

Proposed information architecture

A proposal, not a decision. Six groups instead of eight peers, ordered everyday first. Two moves do most of the work: every visual choice collects under Appearance, and the per-provider on/off switches fold into the Accounts group they belong to. Every option below assumes this map.

Layout options

Eight shapes for the same content, each drawn at its own natural width. Options at 500pt or narrower are shown as a dark / light pair; the three widest (A, D and H) follow the Dark / Light control at the top of the page, because two of those windows will not sit side by side in this document. The pairs follow the control too: the selected scheme is always on the left and its opposite on the right.

A Sidebar navigation 720 × 660

The System Settings idiom: six destinations in a sidebar, one pane at a time, no scroll for most of them.

Fixes callouts 1, 2 and 5 outright. The sidebar is the map, the "Preferences" and "Sources" list groups state the hierarchy in words, and Appearance finally holds every visual choice in one pane where the swatch grids get 484pt of width instead of 420pt. Costs the window is 260pt wider and the user now has to know which pane a setting lives in. Appearance is the tallest pane and sets the height at 660pt; General and Menu Bar are far shorter, so those panes sit in obvious dead space unless the window resizes per pane. Feasibility NavigationSplitView with a List(selection:) sidebar and Section headers, .navigationSplitViewColumnWidth(196), detail panes reusing the current tiles unchanged. This is the least custom option in the set.

B Toolbar icon tabs 500 × 428

Classic macOS Preferences: six icon tabs across the top, the window resizes to whichever pane you pick.

Dark
Light

Fixes the same hierarchy and wayfinding problems as A while keeping the window narrow, and the tab row is a genuinely faster switch than a sidebar for six items. Costs six labels in 500pt means 83pt per tab, which is tight for "Appearance" and forces "API Spend" to abbreviate at smaller widths. The idiom also reads slightly dated next to System Settings, and a resizing window is jarring if the user moves between panes often. Feasibility TabView with .tabViewStyle(.grouped) on macOS 15 gives this natively, including the toolbar chrome and the per-pane window sizing. Icons need real SF Symbols rather than the traced shapes drawn here.

C Grouped single scroll with a jump bar 460 × 700

Keep the scroll, give it a map: a sticky jump bar under the header and named groups between the tiles.

Dark
Light

Fixes callouts 1, 2 and 5 at the lowest cost of any option: nothing moves windows, the merged Appearance tile collects Theme, meter style and Percentages, and "Preferences" / "Sources" separators finally rank the groups. Everything stays visible to a scroll, which suits a settings surface people visit twice a year. Costs it is still a 1,300pt scroll, so the jump bar is a patch over the length rather than a cure. A sticky segmented control inside a settings window is also not a macOS pattern, and five 84pt segments are cramped. Feasibility ScrollViewReader plus scrollTo(_:anchor:) on section ids, with the jump bar pinned outside the ScrollView. Straightforward, but the jump bar is custom chrome that will need its own keyboard story.

D Preview-first studio 760 × 560

A pinned live specimen on the left, the visual controls on the right. Every choice is answered on screen.

Fixes callout 6, which no other option really solves. The user picks Machined + Glass and watches a real Claude card and the menu bar strip change together, which is the only honest way to choose between five fills. It also proves the shared-decision principle: one style, three surfaces, one screen. Costs it is the widest window in the set and it earns its width only in Appearance and Menu Bar. Accounts and API Spend have nothing to preview, so the left pane either goes blank or shows something irrelevant, and a settings window that changes shape per section is worse than one that does not. Feasibility HSplitView or a fixed HStack with the specimen in a .safeAreaInset, driven by the same MeterStyle binding the popover reads. The specimen is literally ProviderCardView and MenuBarLabel with fixture data, so it stays truthful as the renderer evolves. Best treated as the Appearance pane of A rather than as a whole window.

E Display | Sources split 480 × 640

One decision at the top: are you changing how Headroom looks, or where its data comes from.

Dark
Light

Fixes the hierarchy problem with one control instead of six, and it names the real split in this app: four tiles you touch to taste, two you touch when something breaks. Each half then fits close to one screen, so the scroll stops being the main experience. Costs two segments hide six sections, so "which half is Widgets in" is a guess until you learn it. It also gives Accounts, the most complex surface in the window, no top-level presence. Feasibility a Picker(.segmented) bound to an enum above a ScrollView that switches content, with .transition(.opacity) between halves. Cheap to build and cheap to reverse, which makes it a reasonable half-step toward A.

F Grouped inset lists 460 × 700

The Sequoia Form idiom: section titles outside the container, hairline-separated rows inside it.

Dark
Light

Fixes the material problem by admitting these are lists, not cards. Titles move outside the container, so the tile stops carrying a heading it never needed, and hairline separators do the work that 10pt gaps were doing with less ink. It is the densest option here and by some distance the most native on Sequoia. Costs it is the one option that changes the rhythm on purpose: 10pt row padding inside a separated list is tighter than the current 10pt gap between free rows, and the meter pickers have to compress into a single inline swatch row, which loses the two-state (62% healthy over 38% low) fill comparison. Accounts rows, which are two lines tall with badges and status, fight the list grid. Feasibility Form with .formStyle(.grouped) gives all of this for free, including the section titles and separators, and would delete a lot of the hand-built layout in SettingsView. The custom swatch grids need LabeledContent wrappers to sit correctly in a grouped form.

G Accordion with summaries 440 × 840 open · 450 collapsed

Every section collapses to one line that states its current value, so the closed window is a status report.

Dark
Light

Fixes the scroll completely in the state that matters: collapsed, all six sections sit in about 450pt with nothing to scroll, and the summaries ("Micro · Chip · Color as event", "Claude 2, Codex 1, Grok 1, Cursor 1") answer most visits without opening anything. That is a real match for a surface people open to check a value, not to change one. Costs every change is now two clicks, and disclosure state is a thing to remember and get wrong. Summaries also have to stay true: a wrong one is worse than none, and "Anthropic key saved · Current month" quietly omits two other providers. Only one section can be open comfortably, so cross-section work (meter style and menu bar together) gets harder, and opening Appearance pushes the window to 840pt, taller than today’s 700pt. Feasibility DisclosureGroup with a custom label, or LabeledContent in a grouped Form if the summaries stay text-only. Cheap. The summary strings are the actual work, and each is a new piece of derived state to keep in sync.

H Gallery-led appearance 520 × 620

Six named presets as real mini cards, with the three-axis picker demoted behind Customize.

Fixes the actual decision problem. Format × shape × fill is 18 valid combinations, and asking someone to assemble one from three abstract axes is a worse question than asking them to point at the one they like. Named presets ("Quiet", "Instrument", "Signal") also give the choice a vocabulary, and every card is a real provider card in that style, so the preview is the picker. Costs presets are new persisted product surface: names to maintain, a "Custom" state when the axes drift off a preset, and a migration for anyone whose current combination is not one of the six. The gallery also scrolls horizontally in a 520pt window, which hides two of the six until you drag. Feasibility a ScrollView(.horizontal) of MeterGauge-rendered cards plus a DisclosureGroup holding today's MeterStyleTile. No new model beyond a MeterStyle preset table, since a preset is just a named triple. ViewThatFits can drop the gallery to two rows in narrow windows.

Recommendation

Ship C now, then A

C is the honest first move. It fixes the three problems that actually cost people time (no hierarchy, split visual choices, no wayfinding) without moving a single control into a new window shape, and it can go out in one pass. Merging Appearance, meter style and Percentages into one tile is most of the win on its own, and the "Preferences" / "Sources" separators do the ranking that eight peer tiles refuse to do.

A is where it should end up. Once Accounts routinely holds five Claude rows plus three other providers, one scroll stops working at any length, and a sidebar is the native answer macOS users already know. Doing C first is not wasted: the merged Appearance tile and the regrouped Accounts become A's panes unchanged.

Skip B, E and G as destinations. B is a narrower A with worse labels. E hides six sections behind two words. G optimises for reading values, which is what the popover and the menu bar are already for.

Pickers, and what to defer

Treatment (i), the refined swatch grid. Comparison fairness is the first product principle, and only the grid shows all four shapes and all five fills at once at the same size, on one well, at the same severity pair. Segmented rows make the user hold four unseen options in their head, and a preset gallery quietly ranks six combinations above the other twelve.

Adopt D's specimen as a component, not as a window. Put the live provider card at the top of the merged Appearance tile, above Theme. It answers callout 6, it reuses ProviderCardView so it stays truthful, and it costs about 90pt of height in a window that has plenty.

Defer: the preset gallery (H) until there is evidence people find the three axes hard, since presets add persisted product surface and a Custom state; the slider, permanently; and F's grouped Form, which is the right long-term material but should not be attempted in the same pass as an IA change, because it would move every row and every separator at once.
