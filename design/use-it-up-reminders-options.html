Headroom / Feature exploration / 19 September 2026

Don’t let it expire.
Five ways to ask for a nudge.

A reminder when a limit is about to reset and you still have plenty left. Opt-in, off by default, with thresholds the user can see. Five shapes for the same feature, from a single switch to an editable runway — all drawn with the Settings window’s real tokens and metrics.

The request. “Feature request: a push notification if you’ve got X% left when you’re close to the reset to remind you to use it up!” — he currently sets calendar reminders like “burn through AI credits, 10am”.

The reply. Opt-in, in Settings, with user-determined % and time-left thresholds.

Then. “Yeah with some sensible defaults seems great to me! Even multiple like: 1 day at X / 6 hours at X.”

01 / Placement

It rides the pane that owns cadence

General already holds the poll interval and Launch at login. A reminder is a cadence preference, so it becomes a third group in the same tile rather than a new room.

02 / The help line is the UI

The defaults are written out

Two rungs and the 5-hour exclusion are stated in one 11pt help line at the shared 460pt measure. Nobody has to open anything to learn what will happen.

03 / Where it stops

No escape hatch

Someone on a 200-a-week plan who wants a nudge at 80% cannot have it. That is the trade: the smallest surface in the set buys the least room to be wrong about someone.

B

Pace presets

A switch and one three-way choice. The read-out underneath spells out the selected preset in the same words the notification will use.

Claude Weekly resets in 1 day

80% left — about a week of normal use. Next reminder at 6 hours.

Balanced pace. Relaxed sends the 1 day reminder only.

The three bundles

Relaxed1 day before, at 60% or more left. One reminder.
Balanced1 day at 50%+, then 6 hours at 25%+.
Eager2 days at 60%+, 1 day at 40%+, then 6 hours at 20%+.
CostOne switch, one segmented picker, one derived string. Three stored bundles in Core, no per-rule persistence.
Best ifYou want people to feel they chose something without giving them numbers to get wrong — and you expect to tune the bundles later without migrating anyone's data.
01 / Pane placement

A second tile in General

Not a new destination. General already carries a second titled tile (Updates) when an updater is configured, so Use-it-up reminders follows an established shape at 28pt tile-to-tile, and the sidebar stays at six rooms.

02 / Segmented, at the shared width

220pt, like every other picker

SettingsMetrics.segmentedMaxWidth is 220 precisely so the eye does not re-learn the trailing control per row. Three cells fit at 12pt without truncating.

03 / The read-out does the teaching

A preset that says what it is

“Balanced” alone is meaningless. The line beneath restates it as thresholds, so switching presets is a legible change rather than a guess.

C

Rule list

The literal request: several rules, each a lead time and a percentage, pre-filled with the two sensible ones. A seventh destination gives future notification types somewhere to live.

Codex Weekly resets in 6 hours

27% left — about a day of normal use. Resets today at 4:00 PM.

now

The second rule (6 hours / 25%) firing on the Codex Pro weekly window.

CostOne new pane, one new row component, a small ordered list in Preferences. Roughly the Menu Bar pane's build.
Best ifYou expect to add more notification types, and you want the two defaults visible as editable rows rather than hidden behind a word.
01 / A room, not a row

Notifications joins the App section

Use-it-up is the first notification Headroom would send, not the last: low-headroom warnings and reset-happened belong here too. A seventh sidebar row costs nothing — the column is already sized for “Widgets & iPhone” at 216pt.

02 / One new component

The inset rule row

Everything else is vocabulary the window already has: switch row, checkbox group, pop-up button, help text, bordered button. The rule row is a recessed well at the shared recessedWell tint with hairline separators — the setup flow's command well, re-used.

03 / Scope stays a checkbox group

Window kinds, not providers

“Weekly limits” and “Billing periods” are the two window kinds the app already models. 5-hour ships off with the reason written under it, so the setting teaches why rather than just refusing.

D

Sentence builder

The same two rules written as English. Scope moves inside the sentence, so the separate “Applies to” group disappears.

Claude Weekly resets in 1 day

80% left — about a week of normal use. Resets Sunday at 9:00 AM.

now

Same notification as A and C. The sentence only changes how the rule is written.

CostC's persistence plus a wrapping inline-control layout. The sentence is the component.
Best ifYou think the rule list will read as a settings grid nobody understands, and you are willing to own a layout that only works in English until someone re-authors it.
01 / Self-documenting

It reads like Mail rules

No legend, no help line explaining what the columns mean. A person who has never opened this pane can read the rule aloud and be right about what it does.

02 / Scope per rule

More expressive than C

“Claude Weekly” and “any billing period” are different sentences rather than one rule set filtered by a global checkbox group — which is the thing C cannot do.

03 / Where it strains

Wrapping and localisation

Three pop-up buttons in one line of prose wrap unpredictably at the window's 720pt floor, and the sentence has to be re-authored for every language rather than re-labelled. Worth checking against the real 13pt metrics before committing.

E

Runway editor

Preview-first, in the spirit of the Appearance specimen and the Menu Bar arranger: the last stretch before a reset drawn as a track, with the reminders sitting on it.

Grok billing period resets in 1 day

84% left — most of the period unused. Resets Thursday at 12:00 AM.

now

Grok and Cursor bill as period pools, so their reminder says “billing period”, not “weekly”.

CostHighest in the set — a bespoke non-linear scale, drag with snapping, a keyboard equivalent, and a live example that must stay honest. Plus C's persistence underneath.
Best ifYou want the reminder feature to be the thing people screenshot — and you have the appetite for a second specimen to maintain alongside Appearance and Menu Bar.
01 / The track is non-linear

The final day gets the room

7 days to reset compresses to the first fifth of the track and the last six hours get a quarter of it, because that is where every decision is. Ticks at 7d, 3d, 1d, 6h and reset keep the distortion legible.

02 / The example line is the proof

Real data, live

“Claude Weekly · 80% left · resets in 1d 15h → first reminder in 15 hours”. Same contract as the Appearance specimen: a setting can never preview as something the app will not do.

03 / Rows under the picture

The track is not the only way in

Dragging is a pointer affordance. The identical rules sit underneath as compact rows, and each marker is a role="slider" that accepts arrow keys, so nothing here is mouse-only.

Shared behaviour

True of every option above. These are the parts worth agreeing on before the shape is picked.

Permission

The first switch-on triggers the macOS notification permission prompt. Nothing is requested before that — an app that asks at launch gets denied at launch.

If permission is denied or later revoked, the row shows an inline notice and a way out. The switch itself goes back to off and dims, because it cannot honestly claim to be on:

Remind me before unused usage resets
Notifications are turned off for Headroom.Open System Settings…

Headroom re-checks authorisation whenever the Settings window opens, so granting permission in System Settings and coming back clears the notice without a relaunch.

Firing rules

  • Each rule fires at most once per reset cycle, per window. Two rules on one window are two notifications, not two per poll.
  • Never fires from .stale or .unavailable data. A retained row with an old observation time is not evidence that the usage is still there.
  • Evaluated on each poll, so accuracy is bounded by the refresh interval — 5 minutes by default, 2 minutes at the floor. “6 hours” means “the first poll at or under 6 hours”, and the copy should never imply better.
  • Thresholds are stated as % left, matching the popover and the menu bar. The app already has two readings of one number; a notification that flipped to “% used” would make it three.
  • A window with no genuine scheduled reset never fires. UsageWindow.resetSchedule is the gate that already exists for this.

Scope honesty

This is a local macOS notification posted by the running menu bar app. If Headroom is not running, or the Mac is asleep, there is no reminder — the same constraint the menu bar label already lives with.

iPhone push is a separate piece of work and should not be implied anywhere in this pane. The iOS companion mirrors the Mac through a shared envelope; it has no server, no push token, and no way to evaluate a rule while the Mac is closed. Delivering the same reminder to a phone means new infrastructure, not a checkbox.

Open question: whether the request — which said “push notification” — is satisfied by a Mac banner at all. Worth asking him before the shape is chosen, because “notification on the Mac where I work” and “notification on my phone” are different features with an order-of-magnitude between them.

Recommendation

A recommendation, not a decision.

Ship C, written with A’s discipline.

A master switch and a pre-filled two-rule list in a new Notifications pane. It is the literal ask — “multiple like: 1 day at X / 6 hours at X” — and it lands in the existing row grammar with exactly one new component, the inset rule row. Everything else is a switch row, a checkbox group, a pop-up button and a help line the window already draws.

Borrow A’s copy discipline for it: the pane opens with the two defaults already in place and one help line, so a person who never touches a pop-up gets A’s experience and never learns there was a list. The 5-hour checkbox ships off with its reason written underneath rather than hidden.

The seventh destination is the part that pays later. Low-headroom warnings, “your weekly limit just reset”, and whatever the iPhone story turns out to be all need a room, and adding one to the sidebar after the fact is a worse edit than adding it now.

E is not wasted. Its runway track is the natural header specimen for C’s tile — a picture above the rules it edits, the way Appearance and Menu Bar already work. Ship C without it, then add the track when the rule shape has stopped moving.

Against B: presets are a good answer to “too many numbers”, but the numbers here are two. Naming them Relaxed and Balanced adds a vocabulary to learn on top of the thresholds, not instead of them. Against D: the sentence reads beautifully and wraps badly; revisit it if C’s rows test as opaque.
