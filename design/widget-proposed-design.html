Headroom widgets — the proposed design

Every widget Headroom draws, on macOS and iPhone, at 1 CSS px = 1 pt, with every recommendation from the craft audit folded in at once. Each family carries switches so you can walk its important states, flip the two open questions, and put today's tile next to the proposed one. Nothing here argues. The arguments, the options and the rejected alternatives live in widget-craft-options.html; every callout on this page links back to its area there.

System font stack only, no external requests, no looping motion. State changes crossfade once at 190 ms and stop; reduced motion removes even that.

What the popover already decided

The macOS popover is the reference surface. Five of its decisions are what this proposal ports to the widgets; one of them deliberately stays behind. The account card below is the shipped craft-round-2 recipe at full size, beside the small widget at full size, in the same scheme at the same scale.

Small

One header, one line per provider, no labels and no footer, so the header is the only thing that says the number is remaining, and ink is the only freshness cue. The row is a name line with the numeral setting its line box, then the bar on the same axis the name starts on.

Six states at once, 1:1

Calm, Low, Critical, Stale, Attention and Just reset drawn together at true size, so the one red thing is visible without clicking. Follows this family's platform, appearance and name ink.

Medium

Two providers, two columns, one comparison. D2 is four decisions in one tile: each column reserves its own numeral column so both right edges agree (F2), each column mirrors the other's bar axis (F3), the centre rule stays because even 28 pt of gutter cannot separate two columns on its own (L4), and the countdown comes back where it fits (L9).

Dialed dividers, adopted 2026-09-11. The gutter opens to 28 and the iPhone margin to 16; the centre rule is cut back to the meter band — cap top of the first label to the bottom of the last bar in the taller column — and drawn at one device pixel, so each bar ends 14 pt clear of it and it no longer crosses the footer. The spend hairline is gone: 14 pt of air separates the spend row instead, and its amounts are pinned at their measured width so the stamp drops whole rather than letting the figures overlap. The before is in Medium alternatives.

Six states at once, 1:1

Large

The density tier is implicit in the state: Single provider is roomy, Everyday is regular, Dense content is compact. Tiers change spacing and bar height only, so there are two type steps, not four. The inter-provider rule is gone; air separates, and the edge gaps are weighted 0.6 × the provider gap so the tile gains a frame instead of floating in one.

Six states at once, 1:1

Status cards

One copy table, sentence case, real control names, and a Vessel whose fill means something: empty when there is no reading yet, half full when a reading exists but nothing is refreshing it. The text starts on the family's own content axis, with no extra card padding. Small prints the message only.

Design system reference

The values a builder would read: the type ladder, the ink and fill tiers in both schemes, the spacing table, and the axis diagram. Every hex is the resolved value from the audit's colour table, not a guess.

What was folded in

Every recommended option from the craft audit, one line each. The two Discuss items — L9 countdown on medium and D1 name ink, are the two switches above, defaulting to the recommended value.

Medium alternatives

Decided 2026-09-11: B was adopted and is now the medium above. A is kept here as the alternative that was not taken, and the third tile is the previous medium, still drawn, so the before survives.

Four things made the old tile feel cramped. The full-height vertical rule crossed the spend hairline, so the tile read as a T of four boxes instead of one surface. Each meter costs two stacked bands (a label row and a bar), which puts five bands in the Claude column against two in Codex inside 142 pt. The bottom band fused two spend amounts and the stamp onto one 24 pt line under a rule, with 14 pt left to the tile edge. And every bar stopped ~10 pt short of the rule, so the gutter read as leftover rather than structure.

A · Micro rows (not taken) — six states at once, 1:1

Calm, Low, Critical, Stale window, Provider needs attention and Just reset, drawn together at true size. Follows the strip's platform, appearance and name ink.

B · Columns, dividers dialed (adopted) — six states at once, 1:1

The same six states on the adopted layout: rule shortened to the meter band, spend hairline gone. These are the same tiles the Medium section draws.

The trade, and why B won on 2026-09-11. A buys its air with bar length: 113.5 pt on the Mac against 128 in B and 132 in the previous tile. On the 6.1" phone A's countdown column drops whole, and where four meter rows plus the spend line will not fit A drops its least urgent sub-row, which costs a window the column layout can still show. B keeps the bar runs and every window, and pays by keeping two bands per meter: the density is unchanged, only the rules and the gutter stop carving the tile. A stays here as the alternative that was not taken.
